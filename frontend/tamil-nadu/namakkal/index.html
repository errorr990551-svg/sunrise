Selection Protocol

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for vehicle, rig and tanker builders

01

Air, hydraulic fluid or water?

Air and hydraulic lines on rigs and trucks usually run at higher pressure and need strong, correctly threaded parts in heavy-wall MS or suitable stainless. Water and tanker lines suit stainless, which won't rust or contaminate. Tell us the fluid and working pressure.

02

How much vibration?

Rigs, compressors and trucks shake constantly on rough roads. Threaded joints can loosen over time, so tell us the application and assemble with a suitable locking or sealing compound where procedures allow.

03

Which thread?

Domestic equipment mostly uses BSP or BSPT. Imported compressors and rig hydraulic components may use American NPT or JIC standards. BSPT and NPT are not interchangeable. Send a photo or part number.

04

One-off repair or a build run?

Fleet workshops need assorted sizes fast to get trucks back on the road. Builders need repeat batches that match exactly. Tell us which, and we will quote accordingly.

Operational Realities

What Namakkal's Vehicles and Rigs Do to a Nipple

Five realities of a transport and rig town

Constant road vibration

Diesel engines, rig compressors and rough highways loosen threaded joints. Use correct thread engagement, suitable thread-locking paste and pipe support brackets.

Dust, mud and rain

Chassis fittings collect grit and highway water. Bare mild steel rusts rapidly. Stainless or properly coated parts remain serviceable year after year.

Operating heat and ambient sun

Compressors, hydraulic tanks and summer tarmac heat fittings up. Verify that your thread sealant carries an appropriate thermal rating.

High volume and urgent turnaround

A fleet repair garage may need a dozen sizes in a single morning. Standard assortments keep lorries moving without waiting on piece-by-piece orders.

Poultry farm ammonia

Egg-producing sheds use water supply lines where ambient ammonia attacks ordinary metals. Stainless or brass fittings provide long-term service.

Fleet Maintenance

Fitting Nipples on Vehicles, Rigs and Tankers

Habits that stop joints working loose

  • Use the right thread sealant or thread-locker: Match the compound to fluid pressure, vibration levels, and temperature.
  • Don't over-tighten into cast iron or aluminum: Heavy torque can crack cast air receivers or strip threads.
  • Support cantilevered runs: Long barrel nipples carrying heavy brass ball valves act as levers under vibration; add a bracket.
  • Use flexible hose sections: Install flexible hose jumpers between vibrating engines/compressors and rigid chassis pipework.
  • Clean old grit from threads: Wipe away highway dust and hardened sealant before refitting replacement nipples.
  • Re-torque after initial test runs: Check joint tightness after the first few operating hours of road or drilling trials.
  • Pressure-test before releasing vehicles: Follow standard shop safety procedures before returning trucks or rigs to active highway service.
