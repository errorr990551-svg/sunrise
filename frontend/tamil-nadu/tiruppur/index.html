A Knitwear Capital That Runs on Water, Steam and Chemistry

Why Tiruppur's pipework is harder on nipples than most cities'

Tiruppur is India's best-known knitwear exporter. Around the city and across Palladam, Avinashi, Perumanallur and Veerapandi, hundreds of units knit, dye, bleach, print and stitch garments for export. The garment factories themselves use steam presses, boilers and compressed air. The processing units use far more: large volumes of hot water, steam and dye liquor, then treatment plants for the effluent.

That makes Tiruppur an unusual market for a nipple supplier. Most cities buy nipples for water, air and general utilities. Tiruppur's dye houses buy them for liquor that is hot, salty and alkaline, and for steam lines that cycle between hot and cold every shift. Effluent treatment adds acids, alkalis and oxidising chemicals. Units here also work under strict rules on how they treat and discharge water, so treatment plants run round the clock and a leak is an operating problem, not just a maintenance one.

When the metal is chosen well, the nipple lasts for years. When it isn't, you're back in the shed every few months.

Engineering Decisions

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for dyeing, steam and effluent lines

01

Is there salt or chloride in the fluid?

Reactive dyeing uses salt and soda ash, so the liquor carries chlorides and alkali. Chloride is what attacks 304 first. For these lines, 316 or 316L is the safer choice. For soft water, cooling water and clean air, 304 is enough and costs less.

02

How hot, and at what pressure?

Steam and hot-liquor lines need a thicker wall and the right sealant. Schedule 80 is usually the sensible choice for steam. Tell us the operating temperature and pressure and we'll recommend, or ask your process engineer to specify.

03

Which thread does the equipment use?

Imported dyeing and finishing machines often use different threads from local pipework. BSPT and NPT are not interchangeable. Send a photo or the machine's part number.

04

Which parts should you stock?

Pick the sizes that fail or get replaced most during shutdowns. Send your line sizes and we'll help you list a spares kit, so a leak doesn't stop an entire dyeing lot mid-shift.

Dyehouse Pitfalls

What Tiruppur Processing Does to a Nipple

Five problems we see, and what to do about each

Salt and alkali in the liquor

The salt used in reactive dyeing keeps chloride levels high. In wet, warm conditions, 304 can pit and leave rust-coloured weeping at the thread. Use 316 or 316L.

Hot chloride and stress cracking

Austenitic stainless steels can crack under tensile stress in hot chloride environments. Avoid over-tightening, maintain correct thread engagement, and discuss operating temperatures.

Thermal cycling on steam lines

Steam lines heat and cool every shift, causing continuous expansion and contraction. A loose thread weeps first, then leaks. Use a high-temperature sealant and re-torque after initial heat cycles.

One leak, one stopped line

A failed fitting can stop a dyeing machine mid-lot, spoiling fabric value. A small spares kit of common nipples costs far less than a single ruined lot.

Effluent plant chemicals

ZLD dosing lines carry acids, alkalis and oxidising chemicals. Confirm grade compatibility for every dosing line before installation.

Fitment Protocol

Fitting Nipples on Hot, Wet and Chemical Lines

Habits that cut leaks and re-work

  • Use the right sealant: PTFE tape suits many water and air joints, but steam lines need a high-temperature compatible compound.
  • Don't over-tighten stainless: Excess force can gall threads or crack cast valves. Tapered threads seal by geometry, not bruteforce torque.
  • Use anti-seize on reusable joints: Essential on filter, strainer, and pump connections that are opened during regular cleaning shifts.
  • Re-check after the first heat cycles: Thermal expansion on new steam lines often requires an initial check after the first shift.
  • Isolate dissimilar metals: Avoid connecting stainless to galvanized fittings in wet, chemical environments to eliminate galvanic corrosion.
  • Support long runs: Machine vibration from centrifuges and winches shakes loose unsupported pipe joints.
  • Label thread types: Mark BSPT and NPT lines clearly on machine headers.
