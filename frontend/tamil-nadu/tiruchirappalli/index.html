Quality Criteria

Four Questions That Decide Whether a Nipple Is Accepted

Written for specification-driven buyers

01

What exactly does specification say?

"SS nipple" isn't a specification. Send the grade, standard if any, wall thickness, thread type and size, length, tolerance and marking requirements. Referenced standards for stainless pipe nipples usually include ASTM A733 for the nipple and ASME B1.20.1 for NPT thread form.

02

What documentation do you need?

Inspectors may want a material test certificate and traceable heat marking. Tell us before you order, and we'll confirm what accompanies the material batch.

03

Does design code allow threaded joint?

For critical high-pressure or high-temperature lines, design codes may call for welded or flanged connections instead. Threaded nipples are common on instrument and small-bore connections. We supply to your drawing, and the design decision stays with your engineer.

04

Which thread, and how is it gauged?

NPT and BSPT are not interchangeable, and threaded parts are usually checked with ring and plug gauges. Send your thread standard and inspection method, and we'll plan around it.

Five realities of supplying a specification-driven city

Same part, every time

Boiler-ancillary and railway buyers re-order the same fittings. Dimensions and threads must stay identical across lots. Share your acceptance criteria so our inspection matches.

Paperwork is part of the product

A good nipple with no traceability can fail the client audit. Clarify what documents you need at enquiry stage, not after dispatch.

Heavy wall means different stock

Schedule 80 nipples need thicker pipe stock and more careful threading. If you need it in volume, tell us early.

Imported instruments

Pressure gauges, transmitters and valves often come with NPT threads while the plant runs BSP. Mixed-thread nipples bridge the gap.

Procurement cycles can be long and delivery schedules fixed. Tell us the dates you're working to, and we'll tell you honestly if we can meet them.

Assembly Protocol

Fitting Nipples on Instrument and Utility Lines

Habits that survive inspection and re-torque

  • Use the specified sealant: Note the approved chemical compound on the job inspection report.
  • Clean threads before assembly: Completely clear cutting oil, dirt, and swarf from threads and bores.
  • Don't over-tighten: Tapered threads seal by wedging, and stainless can gall. Use a measured torque wrench where procedures give one.
  • Use anti-seize compound: Essential on stainless threads that will be opened during routine inspection cycles.
  • Support instrument lines: Secure impulse piping so equipment vibration doesn't loosen threaded joints.
  • Mark thread types: Tag BSPT and NPT lines clearly on manifold blocks.
  • Gauge check prior to installation: Verify thread pitch and crest engagement with calibrated plug/ring gauges where procedures require.
