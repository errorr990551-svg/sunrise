Salem is home to the Salem Steel Plant, a SAIL unit that makes stainless steel flat products, and a whole ecosystem has formed around it: utensil and cookware makers, stainless furniture shops, pipe and tube fabricators, polishing units and kitchen-equipment builders. Strategic Buying

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for a market that already knows stainless

01

Which grade will your customers accept?

Salem buyers know that cheaper grades exist and sometimes ask for them. For threaded nipples in wet or hot service, we steer you to 304 as the reliable baseline, because thread roots are where weak grades fail first. For salt, chemicals and outdoor use, choose 316.

02

What's the thread?

BSP and BSPT dominate local plumbing. Imported equipment brings NPT. They don't swap, even when the nominal inch size looks identical. Send us the fitting or a photo.

03

What's your sales pattern?

Dealers should tell us their top ten sizes and typical monthly volume, so we can quote bulk assortments. Fabricators should send annual quantities and component drawings.

04

How should it be packed?

Tell us if you need counted boxes, barcode-labelled bags for showroom shelves, or loose bulk master bags for fabrication workshops.

Installation Advice

Fitting SS Nipples in Kitchens, Tanks and Plants

Habits for fitters and plumbers

  • Use quality thread sealant: PTFE tape or anaerobic paste on tapered threads ensures a durable seal without weeping.
  • Hold the nipple by its hex flat: Tightening a wrench against the thread crests strips the mating surface.
  • Don't over-tighten: Stainless threads can gall under dry friction, and over-torquing can crack cast bronze or iron pump bodies.
  • Use anti-seize compound: Recommended on any stainless-to-stainless threaded joint that will be dismantled during maintenance.
  • Match thread families: Never force a tapered BSPT nipple into an NPT threaded port.
  • Support heavy cantilevered pipe runs: A long barrel nipple holding a heavy gate valve acts as a lever and should be braced.
