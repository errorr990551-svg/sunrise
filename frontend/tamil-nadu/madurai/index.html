Engineering Checklist

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for hotels, kitchens and building-services teams

01

Does fluid touch food or drinking water?

Kitchen, drinking-water and food-contact lines call for clean stainless, usually 304. Avoid mixing in galvanised or painted steel in those lines, which can flake and stain.

02

How hot is it?

Hot-water, laundry and sterilisation lines run hot. Thicker-wall Schedule 80 suits steam and higher-pressure service. Tell us the temperature and pressure, or ask your engineer to specify.

03

Which thread does equipment use?

Madurai kitchens and laundries run imported and local equipment side by side, and BSPT and NPT don't mix. Send a photo or the equipment part number.

04

How many sizes do you need at once?

Fitting out a hotel or hospital wing means a long list of sizes. Send the whole bill of materials, and we'll quote and pack it as one consolidated order.

Operational Factors

What Madurai Building Services Face

Five realities of hospitality and hospital plumbing

Hygiene first

Kitchens, laundries and hospitals can't tolerate rust staining, flaking or scale-shedding fittings. Stainless keeps lines clean and contaminant-free.

Hard water and scale

Hard water is common across southern Tamil Nadu, creating scale in hot-water tanks. Stainless tolerates acid descaling chemicals without being destroyed like galvanised steel.

Festival and season peaks

The Chithirai festival in April and May brings heavy crowds. Hotels refurbish and top up spares beforehand. Order early so plumbers aren't delayed during maintenance.

Hidden plumbing shafts

Many pipes run behind tiled walls and acoustic ceilings. A failed nipple means tearing down expensive finishes. Use the right grade the first time.

A long way from the source

Madurai is far from Ahmedabad. Consolidate your project quantities and complete bills of materials so everything dispatches in one coordinated load.

Installation Advice

Fitting Nipples in Kitchens, Laundries and Plant Rooms

Habits for building-services teams

  • Use temperature-rated sealant: PTFE tape suits cold-water runs. Steam and solar hot-water lines demand high-temperature pipe sealant.
  • Hold by the hex: Never grip threads with a pipe wrench; surface teeth strip the thread crowns.
  • Don't over-tighten: Tapered threads seal by geometry wedging. Heavy torque can crack cast bronze bodies or gall stainless steel.
  • Keep site spares: Maintain a small box of common sizes on-site for immediate repair without room downtime.
  • Isolate dissimilar metals: Stainless in contact with galvanized iron in wet conditions will induce accelerated galvanic corrosion.
  • Pressure-test before closing walls: Test lines to rated pressure before closing drywall or tiling service shafts.
  • Label hidden service valves: Clear tags save maintenance hours during urgent emergency shut-offs.
