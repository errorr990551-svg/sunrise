Industry in and around Mysuru is more varied than the tourist picture suggests. The Hebbal, Hootagalli and Belagola industrial areas house engineering, electronics and general manufacturing units. Buying Guide

Questions to Ask Before You Order in Mysuru

Tailored specifically for Mysuru's industrial conditions

01

Does the line touch food?

Utility lines around food plants and kitchens need clean stainless with smooth, burr-free threads. Avoid painted, galvanised or plated fittings that can flake. Product-contact piping usually uses sanitary fittings, and nipples serve the utility side.

02

What cleaning chemicals are used?

Food plants and hospitals clean with caustic, acid and sometimes chlorine-based sanitisers. Chlorides can pit 304 over time, so use 316 on those lines and rinse well.

03

Is it a hidden joint?

Hotels, hospitals and apartment blocks bury pipes behind finishes. A failed fitting there is expensive. Stainless is cheap insurance.

04

What thread does the equipment use?

Imported packaging and process machinery often comes with NPT, while local pipework uses BSPT. They don’t interchange. Send a photo or the part number.

Metallurgy Guide

SS 304, SS 316, MS or Brass: Mysuru Edition

Match the material to the line environment, not just the upfront budget

Material Works Well When… Think Twice When…
SS 304 Food-plant utilities, hot water, steam, hotel plumbing Chlorine-based sanitiser lines
SS 316 / 316L Dosing, cleaning-chemical and harsher lines Cost-sensitive plain lines
Brass Compressed air, low-pressure water Steam with alkali, ammonia
Mild steel Dry painted air and coolant lines Wet, hygienic or hidden lines

In food plants, hotels and hospitals, avoiding rust and flaking is critical. SS 304 provides the clean baseline, while SS 316 protects chemical dosing lines.

Field Fitting Advice

Fitting Stainless Nipples in Mysuru Without Headaches

Practical field habits that save plant hours and prevent leaks

🔧

Tip 1

Use a food-safe sealant on food-adjacent lines. Steam lines need a high-temperature compound.

🔧

Tip 2

Use anti-seize on stainless threads you open for cleaning.

🔧

Tip 3

Don’t over-tighten. Stainless can gall, and damaged threads trap residue.

🔧

Tip 4

Wipe off machining oil before commissioning clean utilities, and flush.

🔧

Tip 5

Keep carbon-steel tools away from stainless surfaces.

🔧

Tip 6

Pressure-test before closing walls and ceilings.

🔧

Tip 7

Label thread types on mixed BSPT and NPT lines.
