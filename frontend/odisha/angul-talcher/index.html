Buying Guide

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for mine, power, smelter and project buyers

01

What are the temperature and pressure?

Steam and hot-water lines need a heavier wall, the right grade and a sealant rated for the temperature. Send the figures, and ask your design engineer to confirm what a threaded joint may carry.

02

Is it in slurry or dust?

Ash slurry and coal dust are abrasive and wear fittings from inside. Choose a sturdy wall and inspect on a schedule.

03

Is it for a project or an overhaul?

Project piping needs full lists and documentation. Overhauls need parts on site before the window opens. Send the list and the date, and we'll say honestly what we can meet.

04

What does the process specification say?

Gasification, ammonia and chemical lines follow your designer's material rules. We supply to the specification, and we'll say plainly if a duty goes beyond what we offer.

Never use brass or copper alloys on ammonia or syngas lines. High-temperature steam and abrasive fly-ash slurries require Schedule 80 heavy-wall SS 304 or SS 316.

Local Realities

What Coal Dust, Fly Ash and 44-Degree Heat Demand

Five realities of a coal-and-power belt

Heat above 44 degrees

Summer heat expands pipework and bakes sealants. Use sealants rated for the temperature and re-check joints after hot spells.

Coal dust and fly ash

Fine, abrasive dust grinds into threads and wears fittings. Wipe clean before assembly, and keep spares sealed.

Overhaul and project calendars

Planned overhauls and project milestones have fixed dates, and missing small parts delay work. Send lists weeks ahead.

Abrasive slurries

Ash and coal slurries wear small-bore fittings quickly. Inspect and replace on a schedule.

A very long haul from Ahmedabad

Angul and Talcher are far from Gujarat. Holding a few extra fast-moving sizes is cheap insurance.

Maintenance Tips

Fitting Nipples in Dust, Heat and Overhaul Schedules

Habits for overhaul and project crews

  • Clean threads before every assembly. Dust and old sealant cause leaks.
  • Use a high-temperature sealant on steam lines, and anti-seize on stainless threads.
  • Don't over-tighten. Tapered threads seal by wedging, and heat adds stress.
  • Allow for expansion with supports or flexible sections.
  • Use only the materials your process specification allows on ammonia and chemical lines.
  • Record the grade and thread of replaced parts for the next audit.
  • Keep spares sealed and dry in a clean store.
