Regional Analysis

Jute, Old Boilers and New Warehouses

Why this belt buys nipples for century-old plant and brand-new depots

The Hooghly river towns grew around jute. Mills at Serampore, Rishra, Champdani, Bhadreswar and nearby spin and weave raw jute into hessian, sacking and carpet backing, and the process needs steam for batching and softening, water for processing, compressed air and a lot of mechanical power. Many mills operate old boilers and prime movers, and many parts of their piping are original. Uttarpara, Konnagar and Chandannagar add engineering workshops, small foundries, paper units and the dwindling remains of older industry. At Dankuni, the Kolkata–Delhi highway and the rail freight corridor have made the area a magnet for logistics parks, FMCG depots and cold-chain facilities. Inland, Arambagh and Pursurah anchor a district that produces potatoes in enormous volume, with cold storages by the hundred.

So the belt has three needs. Old mills need worn-sample copies for old equipment and honest advice on what will last another decade. New warehouses and cold chains need clean, quickly delivered stainless in volume. Potato cold storages need ammonia-aware advice.

Buying Guide

Four Questions Before You Order

Written for mills, depots and cold-store buyers

01

How old is the machine?

Many mills run original equipment from decades ago. Send the worn nipple or a measured sketch with thread pitch and length, and we’ll machine a match.

02

Is it steam, and at what pressure?

Boiler and batching lines need a heavier wall and a high-temperature sealant. Send the figures, and ask your engineer to confirm what a threaded joint may carry.

03

Is it an ammonia line?

Never use brass or copper alloys on ammonia. Follow your refrigeration designer’s specification, and we’ll supply to it.

04

Do you need a full list?

Warehouses and logistics parks buy dozens of sizes at once. Send the list from your piping drawing, and we’ll quote it as one order.

Local Realities

What Old Mills and River Air Demand

Five realities of the Hooghly’s mill towns

Century-old plant

Old boilers, engines and pipework need careful matching. Custom machining from a sample keeps them running.

Jute-season peaks

Raw jute arrives in the monsoon and mills run flat out afterwards. A steam failure then is expensive.

Lint and dust

Jute fibre and dust clog threads and air lines. Clean threads before assembly, and fit filters.

Humid, flood-prone riverside

Monsoon humidity rusts mild steel quickly, and low-lying areas can flood. Stainless and sealed threads recover better.

New depots, fast schedules

Dankuni’s warehouses fit out on tight dates. Send lists early.

Maintenance Tips

Fitting Nipples in Hooghly–Serampore Plants

Habits that save downtime, prevent callbacks and ensure leak-free runs

  • Use a high-temperature sealant on steam lines, and anti-seize on stainless threads.
  • Use only the materials your refrigeration designer allows on ammonia lines.
  • Don’t over-tighten. Cast housings crack and stainless galls.
  • Clean threads before assembly, especially in lint and dust areas.
  • Allow for expansion with supports or flexible sections.
  • Flush and dry lines after a flood, and replace any rust-affected mild-steel nipples.
  • Label thread types on mixed BSPT and NPT lines.
