Regional Analysis

Paddy, Mustard, Potato and a Hundred Boilers

Why Bardhaman buys nipples for parboilers, expellers and cold stores

Bardhaman district produces rice, potato and mustard in volume, and the economy that grew around those crops is mostly processing. Parboiling rice mills soak, steam and dry paddy before milling, and that needs boilers, steam lines, hot-water tanks and dryers running for long hours. Mustard-oil mills heat seed, press it in expellers and filter the oil. Rice-bran-oil plants extract oil from bran and refine it with steam. Potato cold storages, mostly ammonia-based, hold the crop from harvest through the following months, and puffed-rice, flattened-rice and sweet-making units add small-scale steam and hot-water use. The Panagarh–Kanksa belt on the Durgapur side adds engineering and warehouse units.

Mills run to the seasons. After the harvest the whole district is in motion, and a failed steam fitting stops a mill when it matters most.

That makes spares planning the real service. Boilers and parboilers need the right wall and sealant, cold stores need the right material rules, and everyone needs the part before the rush begins.

Local Realities

What Bardhaman’s Seasons and Mills Demand

Five realities of a paddy and mustard district

Harvest rushes

Paddy, mustard and potato each peak in their season, and mills run flat out. A failed fitting then is costly.

Boilers on long shifts

Daily heat cycles loosen threaded joints over time. Check them on a schedule.

Husk, bran and dust

Rice husk and bran dust clog threads and air lines. Clean before assembly, and keep spares sealed.

Humid, flood-prone canals and rivers

Monsoon humidity rusts mild steel, and the Damodar’s floods have reached low-lying areas. Stainless and sealed threads recover better.

Dealer-led supply

Mills here buy through local dealers who want assortments and reliable reorders.

Maintenance Tips

Fitting Nipples in Bardhaman Plants

Habits that save downtime, prevent callbacks and ensure leak-free runs

  • Use a high-temperature sealant on steam lines, and anti-seize on stainless threads.
  • Use only the materials your designer allows on ammonia and solvent lines.
  • Don’t over-tighten. Heat adds stress to tapered threads.
  • Clean threads before assembly, especially in husk and bran dust.
  • Allow for expansion with supports or flexible sections.
  • Re-check tightness after the first heat cycles and at season start.
  • Keep a small spares kit of the sizes that fail most.
